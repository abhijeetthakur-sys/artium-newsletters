Chinmayee Madha  Student, Artium Academy

An A.R. Rahman fan who brought one of his songs to the unMute stage in Hyderabad, in Telugu, over an arrangement she had never sung before.

  ✦  

Mayya Mayya opens with no music at all. Just a voice, out in the open, with nothing underneath it to hold the pitch. So in the seconds before she started, Chinmayee was humming quietly to herself — worried she might come in on a different scale entirely.

She needn't have worried. "It seems like the venue and the song started to have conversation with me once I started to sing."

She is a serious A.R. Rahman fan, so getting to sing one of his was already the point. But the version waiting for her at unMute was not the one she grew up with. Akshay Purandare had reworked the arrangement, and the first time she heard it she wasn't sure what her voice would do with it. A few run-throughs changed her mind. "It gave the whole new angle to the song, but also keeping the core idea of the song intact."

Then, halfway through, the venue lost power. The team asked her to take it again from the top. By that point the nerves had gone and she knew the room — so the take you are watching is the second one, and it is better for it.

One rehearsal note travelled with her onto the stage. Jaydeep Bagwadkar and her teacher had both told her to sing with more throw. She kept it in mind through the whole song. "It helped me to project me more, the phrases got easier to travel — it helped me understand my voice better."

A note from the Talent Department

Chinmayee told us she used to have stage fright. You would not guess it from this. What she actually did that night was harder than singing well once: she sang half a song, lost the power, and then went back to the top and did it better.

That is the part I keep thinking about. Not the nerves, and not the technical fault, but how quickly she turned an interruption into a second attempt she was glad to get. That is a performer's instinct, and it is not something anyone can teach her in a class.

unMute does not wait for an artist to be famous before giving them a proper stage. That is the whole point. Hyderabad keeps proving it.

Watch it. Share it if it moves you.
