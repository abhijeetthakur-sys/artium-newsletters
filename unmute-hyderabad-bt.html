Bhavya Tumuluru ended her unMute set in Hyderabad with a challenging classical piece. This is how she describes it: “All my 16 years of learning, practice, and hard work came together on this stage through this song.”

The music runs in the family. It started with her great-grandfather, Padma Bhushan and Sangita Kalanidhi Dr. Sripada Pinakapani. Her formal training came under her Gurus, Lalitha ji and Haripriya ji, the Hyderabad Sisters.

Bhavayami Padumente is a song she had always wanted to sing on a big stage. “It has always been one of the inspiring songs for me,” she says. She sang it as a ragamalika, moving through Pantuvarali, Mohana and Suddha Dhanyasi, with Kolipaka Veeraswamy on mridangam and Koundinya Pannala on violin.

Carnatic music usually lives in a sabha. On unMute it shared a stage with reimagined film songs. “Classical music always has its own separate vibe and elegance,” Bhavya says. “Presenting this piece on the unMute stage is a different kind of experience, in front of so many musicians and music lovers.”

Right before she began, the first person on her mind was her Guru, and the Lord. All through the song she found herself recalling moments from class. The part she will hold onto is the ending, “which gives the full finishing note, where it feels like heaven while reaching it.” And the moment she finished? “I just felt, thank God I did it with such a difficult song.”

Bhavayami Padumente

From the Malayalam film Meghatheertham. Saikumar plays Balu Swamy, a proud classical musician who separated from his musician wife years ago. Their grown son, Hari (Siddique), has never forgiven him for abandoning his mother. The characters spend their whole lives unable to say their love, grief and regret to one another, and music is the only language they truly share.

Bhavayami Padumente is that conversation: an intense musical confrontation, full of regret and pride, carrying what forty years of words never could.

A note from the Talent Department

We asked Bhavya what she felt the moment she finished. She didn't say proud. She said, “Thank God I did it with such a difficult song.”

That is the honest answer of someone who knows exactly how hard the thing she just did was. A ragamalika through three ragams, carried live with mridangam and violin, on a stage that also held Hindi and Telugu film songs. She chose to end her set with it: the hardest piece, and the one she had always wanted a big stage for.

Sixteen years of learning sit behind that choice, and a musical line that goes back to her great-grandfather. In Hyderabad, the room got to hear where it leads.

unMute does not wait for an artist to be famous before giving them a proper stage. That is the whole point.

Watch it. Share it if it moves you.

About artium unMute

unMute is Artium Academy's live performance series, built as a format, not a one-off event. It's a recurring show with its own sound, its own city editions, and a roster of artists who return season after season.

Each episode is shot and produced to a fixed format, city by city, so the show scales without losing what makes it work: real performances, minimal gloss, teachers and students sharing the same stage.

unMute is where Artium's talent gets a platform beyond the classroom, and where the audience gets to watch music get made, not just performed.
