Anvitha brought us a shortlist of songs for Hyderabad, and every option on it was interesting. But "Shakkalaka Baby" was the one that stopped us. It's a song built on speed and switches — part melody, part rap, moving through registers most performers twice her age would think twice about taking on live.

What made us curious was simple: how does a student still training in Hindustani classical get her head around something this layered, melodically and lyrically, and make it look easy? We wanted to see if she could actually own it, not just get through it.

It wasn't only a technical question. She had exactly the attitude a song like this actually needs — playful, fearless, entirely unbothered by how complicated it looks from the outside.

She did more than get through it. On stage, she sang it like she'd lived inside the song for years — every switch in rhythm landing like she wrote it herself.

A note from the Talent Department

Anvitha trains with us in Hindustani classical. "Shakkalaka Baby" is about as far from that as a song gets — part melody, part rap, moving fast enough that most performers twice her age would think twice before picking it. She didn't just pick it. She owned it.

Her note from rehearsal was simple: own the song, go full josh. Watching her in Hyderabad, that's exactly what she did — nothing about it looked like a student finding her feet. It looked like someone who'd lived inside the song, with exactly the attitude a song like this needs.

This is what real training buys you: the confidence to walk into a genre you've never formally studied and still land it. Anvitha has a new song for the film Vegaleela releasing this September — Hyderabad was a good preview of what's coming.

unMute does not wait for an artist to be famous before giving them a proper stage. That is the whole point.

Watch it. Share it if it moves you.

About artium unMute

unMute is Artium Academy's live performance series, built as a format, not a one-off event. It's a recurring show with its own sound, its own city editions, and a roster of artists who return season after season.

Each episode is shot and produced to a fixed format, city by city, so the show scales without losing what makes it work: real performances, minimal gloss, teachers and students sharing the same stage.

unMute is where Artium's talent gets a platform beyond the classroom, and where the audience gets to watch music get made, not just performed.
