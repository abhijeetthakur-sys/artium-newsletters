Thanika Varunkumar brought an invocation to London. Mooladhara Moorthy, Papanasam Sivan’s Tamil kriti to Lord Ganesha, sung live at CoLabs - OPEN in Southall on 25 July, on unMute’s first stage outside India.

Rehearsals were harder than the night. “In rehearsal I was stuck worrying about the audience and voice glitches,” she says. On stage, she remembered something else: the goal isn’t perfection. “That one thought cleared the fear in me.”

What she was singing for was simple. “A deep, pure bhakthi to thank Lord Ganesh, Vighnaharta, for his blessings.” And what she held in mind the whole way through: “How to offer my best gratitude properly through the music.”

Asked what the song means to her, she went back to the ground she stands on. “It reminded me to return to my emotional foundation — to find joy in simplicity, trust that obstacles will clear, and lean on faith.”

She describes the stage in three words: “Professionalism met transcendence.” The moment she’ll keep is “the second my entire focus surrounded Ganesh Bappa.” Afterwards, she was “proud I stepped outside my comfort zone, and grateful I had this wonderful opportunity and experience.” And next? “I’m excited for more chances to perform, to learn, and to serve through music, one kriti at a time.”

A note from the Talent Department

Thanika told us that in rehearsal she was stuck worrying about the audience and her voice. Then, on stage, she remembered that the goal isn’t perfection. That one thought cleared the fear.

It is the hardest thing to learn on any stage, and nobody can hand it to you. Thanika found it in front of a room, in a city unMute had never played before, singing a prayer.

London was unMute’s first stage outside India. It feels right that the first song we bring back from there is an invocation.

unMute does not wait for an artist to be famous before giving them a proper stage. That is the whole point.

Watch it. Share it if it moves you.

Abhijeet Thakur

About artium unMute

unMute is Artium Academy's live performance series, built as a format, not a one-off event. It's a recurring show with its own sound, its own city editions, and a roster of artists who return season after season.

Each episode is shot and produced to a fixed format, city by city, so the show scales without losing what makes it work: real performances, minimal gloss, teachers and students sharing the same stage.

unMute is where Artium's talent gets a platform beyond the classroom, and where the audience gets to watch music get made, not just performed.
