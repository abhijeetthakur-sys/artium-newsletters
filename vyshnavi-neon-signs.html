Before You Press Play

We have been watching Vyshnavi build as a singer since she walked in for her first Artium class in June 2024. She is also, as it turns out, an athlete. Someone who competes at NIA level, wins medals, and understands from the inside what it means to push through when everything in you says stop.

Neon Signs is her debut release on Artium Originals. A high-energy English pop anthem, produced by Akshay Purandare and written by Tamoghno Banerjee, about being completely present in a moment: city lights, music loud, not a thought about tomorrow.

About Vyshnavi

Vyshnavi Moola's first Artium class was on 10 June 2024. In the time since, she has done what the best Artium students do: shown up, trained, competed, and kept going.

She is also an athlete. The two sides of her life, the one that competes and the one that sings, turn out to be more connected than they look. Both ask for the same things: discipline, the ability to recover, and the willingness to deliver even when everything in you wants to stop.

She won Artium Superstar Season 2 in the Above 16 category. That win came with a specific prize, the opportunity to record and release an original song through Artium Originals. Neon Signs is that song.

 

The Story Behind the Song

Right before recording, Vyshnavi was at the NIA Pune campus for a week of sports training. She competed, ran, played multiple disciplines, and won five medals. Then she came to Mumbai, walked into The Artium Studios, and started recording a song about being completely present in a moment.

She had five hours of sleep a night that entire week. Sore muscles. An ankle that was still giving her trouble from the sports. None of it showed up in her voice.

What she recorded is a song about exactly that feeling. Being so fully inside a moment that the rest of the noise disappears. The verse says it plainly: "Music loud, Windows down, we're out singing around / City colours are all around / Who's thinking 'bout tomorrow now?" That last line is not a question. It is a declaration.

There is a bridge that slows everything down. The song pulls inward right when you think it is only going to accelerate. When Vyshnavi sang that section, she was thinking of her time in Barcelona as a teenager. Fond memories. Student life. A particular quality of being young in a city with the people you love. Her colour for this song: sparkly golden light. Her time of night: 2:30 am.

The song is for her college girl gang, she says. "This song describes exactly the kind of bond and fun we had. You're always living in the moment when you're with them." The neon signs of the title are the world she built around that feeling.

 

Composed and produced by Akshay Purandare. Written by Tamoghno Banerjee. Recorded at The Artium Studios, Mumbai.

The Song

Neon Signs is structured like the night it describes. The chorus is pure release. "Put your hands up in the Sky / Under the Neon Signs / Say Heyeyey / We're coming alive tonight." The kind of hook that wants volume and a crowd and nowhere else to be. The verse does something different; it opens inward. "Flowing from the sea / Summer breeze on me / Eyes closed in moment / Speeding through my dreams." This is the space inside the night, quieter, more personal, held rather than released.

The bridge lands exactly where it should, slowing everything just long enough to remind you that the best moments do not announce themselves. They just happen, and then they are gone.

 

We're Lost in the moment

Nothing can slow us down now

Vyshnavi's voice carries both sides with ease. The chorus came naturally, fewer takes, immediate energy. The verse required something different: a relaxed, soft tone after the loudness of the chorus, and that shift in dynamics took real concentration. She found it. When she first heard Akshay's produced track, before recording a single note on it, she said it felt groovy. She was right.

"A testament that if you want something bad enough, the universe makes it happen."

A Note from the Talent Development Department

What stayed with me when I heard the recording is that none of that showed up in her voice. She was present. That is not nothing. It is actually the entire point of the song she was recording: to be completely in a moment, to not let the noise get through.
