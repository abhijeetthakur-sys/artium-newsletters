Before You Press Play

A Hindustani classical teacher at Artium. A bhajan Sant Kabir wrote five centuries ago. Recorded, mixed, and mastered start to finish by the artist himself, in his own studio in Kolkata.

Guru Bin Kaun Bataave Baat started as Kaustubh’s own pranam to his Guru, Pt. Anol Chatterjee — and became, in his words, an offering he couldn’t have made any other way.

 

The Origin Story

Kaustubh Bagchi had already spent years learning music by the time he came to Shri Anol Chatterjee. He assumed there wasn’t much more to find. But meeting a Guru, he says, was never really about learning more compositions or sharpening technique — it was about finding direction.

From the very first lesson, Anol ji taught him that music isn’t something you perform; it’s something you live. Every lesson since has circled back to one word: honesty — honesty towards the sur, towards the bandish, and towards himself. “Whatever little understanding I have today of music,” Kaustubh says, “and many of the values I strive to live by, are because of his guidance and blessings.”

Every student, he says, reaches a stage where information becomes abundant but clarity becomes scarce. He had his own moments of questioning his musical choices, his practice, his direction. “Finding Guruji gave me clarity,” he says. “He didn’t just answer questions — he taught me how to ask the right ones.”

The song released on 28 July 2026 on all streaming platforms — on the eve of Guru Purnima, one of the most sacred days on Kaustubh’s calendar.

 

Written by Sant Kabir, a bhajan five centuries old. Composed, produced, mixed, and mastered by Kaustubh Bagchi himself, start to finish, at his own Bagchi Records in Kolkata. Tabla by Arka Banerjee, whose playing supports the composition’s flow without ever overshadowing the poetry or the melody.
 

The Song

Guru Bin Kaun Bataave Baat asks a simple question: who tells you the way, if not your Guru? Kabir builds the poem out of turbulent rivers and towering mountains — the ego, desire, anger, greed, pride, jealousy, and illusion that block a seeker’s path — and answers it with the Guru, the only light that cuts through.

Kaustubh kept the arrangement intentionally minimal. He didn’t want the production to overpower Kabir’s words — every instrument was chosen to create space rather than fill it, so the song would feel less like a produced track and more like sitting in an intimate baithak.

For Kaustubh, the lines about ego hit closest to home. “In music, ego is perhaps the biggest obstacle to growth,” he says. “The day you believe you’ve learned enough is the day your learning stops.”

 

“Humility is not thinking less of yourself — it is simply keeping yourself open to learning.”

— Kaustubh Bagchi

About Kaustubh

Kaustubh Bagchi has been teaching at Artium since March 2022. A Hindustani classical vocalist equally at home in contemporary music, he has become one of Artium’s most sought-after teachers — the kind students specifically ask for.

Being on the other side of the microphone, he says, is its own kind of lesson. “Teaching constantly reminds me to stay accountable to my own learning. Releasing music reminds me what it feels like to be vulnerable — to put your work out into the world.” Guru Bin Kaun Bataave Baat is out now on all streaming platforms.
