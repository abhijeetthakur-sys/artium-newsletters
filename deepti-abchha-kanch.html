Before You Press Play

There are some emotions that only arrive with rain. Abchha Kanch — আবছা কাঁচ, frosted glass — lives inside that suspended, waterlogged moment. Time slows down. A name sits unspoken on your lips. The world outside is all grey and dripping.

It is a Bengali original written and composed by Deepti Mukherjee Mishra, out since 6th August on Spotify, Apple Music and 40+ platforms worldwide. The lyrical video followed a day later.

 

Deepti Mukherjee Mishra

Singer • Composer • Teacher Series

Where the song came from

There was no single incident behind it. Deepti describes it as a feeling many of us carry quietly — "the emotions we never fully express, the words we never send, and the memories that return unexpectedly. Rain somehow became the perfect space for those feelings to exist."

The melody arrived before the words did. "It carried a certain mood that stayed with me, and the words slowly found their place within it," she says. "It felt less like writing and more like listening to what the melody wanted to say."

She has trained in Hindustani classical music since she was three, and that training never leaves her melodic thinking — but she was careful here. "I was careful not to let technique overpower the emotion. I wanted the song to breathe freely while still carrying the depth that my training has given me."

 

About the Song

The lyrics are written in fragments and images rather than straight narrative. They move through frosted glass, the sound of anklets in water, sealed letters, the smell of something unfamiliar — each image a small room the listener can step into.

This is not a love song in the conventional sense. It is something quieter and stranger: the feeling of wanting to disappear with someone, a chest full of blue ache, a water-spirit taking your hand and saying let's go, somewhere far. And underneath all of it, a song about the present moment as refuge.

 

আবছা কাঁচ  /  জল ছোঁয়াচ  /  তোর নামেই তোলা থাক
রাত দুপুর  /  জল নুপুর  /  বর্ষা আর টাপুর টুপুর
বন্ধ খাম  /  সিক্ত ঘাম  /  একলা ঘর এই আরাম
অচেনা ঘ্রাণ  /  বেহায়া গান  /  এই সময় এই বর্তমান...

Abchha kanch / Jol chhoayach / Tor namei tola thak
Raat dupur / Jol nupur / Borsha ar tapur tupur
Bondho kham / Sikto gham / Ekla ghor ei aram
Ochena ghran / Behaya gaan / Ei shomoy ei bortoman...

Frosted glass, and the water's touch — let it all be kept aside, in your name. Deep midnight, anklets of water, the rains and their soft tapping. A sealed envelope, sweat still damp, an empty room and the ease of it. An unfamiliar scent, a shameless song — this time, this present, now.
 

ধুকপুকে সুর বুকে  /  নীল ব্যথার এই বুকে
জলপরী হাত ধরি  /  চল পালাই খুব দূরই..

Dhukpuke sur buke / Neel byathar ei buke
Jolpori haat dhori / Chol palai khub duroi..

A restless tune in the chest, in this chest of blue ache — a water-sprite takes my hand: come, let's run far away.

Of everything in the song, one line stays closest to her — এই সময়, এই বর্তমান. This time, right now, is enough.

She calls it a gentle reminder to be present. "We often live between memories of the past and worries about the future, but peace is usually found in the present moment. That thought has become very meaningful to me, both as an artist and as a person."

 

"It's simple, but it holds the idea of acceptance, hope, and finding beauty in the present. Every time I sing it, it feels honest."

Deepti Mukherjee Mishra
 

In the studio

She handed the song to Anirban Sarkar to produce, and what came back was not quite the version she had been carrying in her head. "He understood the emotional space of the song and added textures that I hadn't imagined but immediately connected with. The final version feels richer than what I had first envisioned, while still staying true to its heart."

The first people she played it for were her closest family. They listened all the way through without saying anything, and then said only: "It feels very real." That was the response she wanted. "I always hope my music connects before it impresses."

 

About Deepti

Born into a family with a deep appreciation for music, Deepti began her journey in Hindustani classical music at the age of three under the guidance of Shri Shashankar Shekhar Bera. She continued her training with Pandit Shri Kumar Mukherjee, the torchbearer of the musical lineage of Ustaad Munabbar Ali Khan Saheeb — son of the legendary Ustaad Bade Ghulam Ali Khan Saheeb of the Kasur Patiala Gharana.

She received advanced training from the Khalifa of Patiala Gharana, Ustaad Raja Ali Khan Saheeb, and studied the art of ghazal under Ustaad Sabir Khan Saheeb, the eminent tabla maestro of the Farrukhabad Gharana. She then spent several years in intensive training, on stage and off, under Pandit Suman Ghosh, a disciple of Padma Vibhushan Sangeet Martand Pandit Jasraj. She continues her musical journey today under Vidushi Anjana Nath.

A vocalist, composer and lyricist, Deepti has already released an original album of songs written and composed entirely by herself — work appreciated for its artistic depth and emotional expression. Abchha Kanch continues that: the traditions she has spent a lifetime inside, carried through her own creative voice.

A Note from the Talent Development Department

Deepti has been with Artium a long time. In that time she has done the thing that a lifetime of classical training makes hardest: she set the technique down far enough to write something this quiet. There is no display anywhere in Abchha Kanch. No moment where the training announces itself. Just rain, a sealed envelope, an empty room, and a line she keeps returning to.

That restraint is the whole point. The Teacher Series exists because there is music inside the people who teach here that never gets recorded — not because it isn't good enough, but because teaching fills the day and the song waits. Deepti didn't let hers wait. Go sit with it in the rain.
